Start with the outside edge of the July 19 map. Large symbols mark positions along a ring. Inside it, small colored points share space with the links between them. The department legend stays at the right. You can read the outline almost immediately. Following a single link through the center takes more effort.

In this excerpt, watch the points closest to the center as the Circle control lights up. They move into bands while the legend holds its place. The recording preserves a detail the poster misses: the points travel to their new positions over several frames, giving the eye time to follow the change.

## Finding a department

Three days later, a still of the force layout shows what happens when the departments have room to separate. Find the green Tools hub near the top. Its neighboring points form a small cluster with a few lines leading away from it. The cyan cluster to its left has many more internal links. Those differences are easier to pick out here than in the rings. The two captures were made on different days, so their on-screen totals describe different snapshots.

## Reading depth from a turn

The sphere needs motion to explain its depth. Pick one of the larger symbols at the edge of the frame below and follow it around. It passes across other points, moves toward the opposite edge, then returns. A still can tell you which points overlap on the screen. The rotation lets you see some of the distance hidden by that overlap.

{% include media.html id="sphere-rotation" %}

The first smooth recording used 120 frames taken from the map canvas at three-degree intervals. A later capture centered the sphere in the frame and extended the turn to eight seconds. Holding the center steady gives the eye a reference point for the moving symbols. The resulting silent clip is the July implementation, preserved at its original pace.
